# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

A small circle of friends, invited with a code. They play mostly on desktop in longer sessions with a mouse and check
in now and then on the phone. They know each other and the game; nobody needs onboarding by strangers' standards.

## Product Purpose

Deep Forge is an idle game: each player runs a dwarf hold cut into a mountain. Dwarves mine, smelt, forge, brew and
trade on a shared market while the server keeps simulating when nobody is looking. Success is a hold you want to come
back to, look at, and tinker with.

## Positioning

The hold is the interface: rooms, dwarves and workshops are clicked directly in a pixel-art cross-section of the
mountain, with windows opening next to what was clicked instead of menus.

## Operating Context

- Server-authoritative Java simulation; the browser client only shows and estimates.
- Vanilla ES modules, no npm and no build step in the frontend.
- Game texts in English, docs and conversation in German.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Tabs: Hold, Mines, Work, Dwarves, Trade, Explore, War, More, opening in a drawer over the scene.
- Windows (sheets) open next to the clicked room or dwarf; quests and expeditions are reports with choices and loot.
- Phone width 390 px must still work: no horizontal scroll, buttons at least 40 px high.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: Deep Forge. Pixel-art scene drawn with pxl; in-canvas text uses the 3x5 pixel font.
- The player asked for a skeuomorphic, dwarven interface: decorated metal windows, paper for quests and expeditions,
  buttons that look like buttons, hourglasses for progress, mechanical slide animations.

## Product Principles

- Click the world, not menus.
- Every warning says what to do.
- Costs are always shown ingredient by ingredient.
